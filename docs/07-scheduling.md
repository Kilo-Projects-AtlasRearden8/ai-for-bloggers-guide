# 07 Scheduling

You do not need to publish every day. A simple, repeatable schedule works better than an ambitious one you cannot keep.

## Step 1: Pick a frequency

Choose one:

- **Once a week** (recommended for beginners) — builds habit, keeps blog fresh
- **Twice a month** — manageable if you're busy
- **Once a month** — minimum to stay alive

Start small. You can publish more often later.

## Step 2: Pick a day and time

Choose a specific day and time, such as "Mondays at 9 a.m." Put it on your calendar or planner.

**Best days for engagement (general):**
- Tuesday–Thursday mornings
- Avoid weekends (lower traffic for most niches)
- Consistency matters more than the exact day

## Step 3: Use your platform's scheduler

Most free blog platforms let you write a post now and set it to publish later:

| Platform | How to schedule |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Blogger** | Post settings → Schedule → Set date/time → Publish |
| **WordPress.com** | Top right: "Publish" dropdown → "Schedule" → Pick date/time |
| **Substack** | Top right: "Publish" dropdown → "Schedule" → Pick date/time |
| **GitHub Pages** | Commit post with future date in front matter; use GitHub Actions or manual deploy |

Write your post ahead of time, set the schedule, and you are done.

## Step 4: Build a buffer

Try to have 2–3 posts written and scheduled before you go public. That way, if life gets busy, your blog keeps running.

**Batch workflow (do once a month):**
1. **Day 1:** Brainstorm 4 topics (Section 2)
2. **Day 2:** Outline all 4 (Section 3)
3. **Day 3–4:** Draft all 4 (Section 4)
4. **Day 5:** Headlines + images for all 4 (Sections 5–6)
5. **Day 6:** Schedule all 4 for the month (this section)

## Simple monthly plan

| Week | Task |
|------|------|
| Week 1 | Draft post, add image, schedule it |
| Week 2 | Draft post, add image, schedule it |
| Week 3 | Draft post, add image, schedule it |
| Week 4 | Rest, or repurpose an old post (see [08 Repurposing](08-repurposing.md)) |

**If you miss a week:** Just pick up next week. The buffer protects you. No guilt.

## Platform-specific scheduling tips

**Blogger:** Use "Set date and time" under Post Settings. Timezone matters — set it to your readers' timezone.

**WordPress.com:** The scheduler uses your site's timezone (Settings → General → Timezone). Double-check it.

**Substack:** Schedules in your account timezone. Good for newsletter + blog simultaneous send.
