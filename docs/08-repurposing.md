# 08 Repurposing

One blog post can become many pieces of content. Repurposing saves time and reaches people who prefer different formats.

## Step 1: Pick a post that did well

Choose a post you already published. It does not need to be famous — just useful.

## Step 2: Turn it into smaller pieces

Ask a free AI:

> "I have a blog post called '[title]'. Turn it into: (1) a 3-sentence social media summary, (2) 3 short tips I can post separately, (3) 3 questions I can ask my readers."

Paste your post text (without personal information) if the AI needs it, or just share the title and main points.

**More repurposing prompts to try:**

> "Turn this blog post into a 5-tweet thread (or 5 Mastodon posts). Each tweet under 280 chars. Include the blog link in the last one."

> "Extract 3 quotable sentences from this post that work as standalone graphics."

> "Rewrite the introduction of this post as a 100-word email newsletter teaser with a 'Read more' link."

> "Create a 3-item checklist from this post that someone could print and use."

## Step 3: Share where your readers are

Post the summary and tips wherever you already spend time:

- A newsletter (Substack, Buttondown, Beehiiv free tiers)
- Social media (Mastodon, Bluesky, Threads, LinkedIn, X)
- A forum or community related to your topic (Reddit, Discord, Facebook Groups)
- A note to friends who might find it useful

## Step 4: Link back

Always link back to the original blog post so new readers can find your site.

## Privacy reminder

Do not repurpose anything that contains personal information. Remove names, addresses, and private details first.

## Worked example: "How to Set Up a Simple Spreadsheet Budget"

**Original post sections:**
1. Why budget with a spreadsheet
2. List your income
3. List your fixed expenses
4. List your variable expenses
5. Track for 30 days
6. Review and adjust

**Repurposed into:**

| Format | Content |
|--------|---------|
| **Mastodon thread (5 posts)** | 1/5 "Budgeting doesn't need an app. A simple spreadsheet works better for many people. Here's how to start → [link]"<br>2/5 "Step 1: List every income source. Use the *lowest* recent amount for variable income. [link]"<br>3/5 "Step 2: Fixed expenses (rent, insurance). These don't change. [link]"<br>4/5 "Step 3: Variable expenses (groceries, gas). Track for 30 days to find your real numbers. [link]"<br>5/5 "Step 4: Review monthly. Adjust. That's it. Full guide: [link]" |
| **3 standalone tips** | 1. "Use your *lowest* recent income for budgeting — not your best month."<br>2. "Track variable spending for 30 days before setting limits."<br>3. "Review your budget on the 1st of every month. 10 minutes max." |
| **Newsletter teaser** | "Most budgeting apps overcomplicate things. This month I built a simple spreadsheet that shows exactly where my money goes — no subscriptions, no sync issues. Here's the template and the 4-step method: [link]" |
| **Printable checklist** | ☐ List all income sources<br>☐ List fixed expenses<br>☐ List variable expense categories<br>☐ Track spending for 30 days<br>☐ Review & adjust on the 1st |
